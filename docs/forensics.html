Correct explanation

Known pixels are masked and do not pay a penalty. H33 deletes a near-catalogue flank, not known pixels. This can reduce FP mass when those off-mask proposals have poor incremental coverage of new truth. For identical truth/masks, pure pruning cannot increase TPw: it can only reduce FPw and preserve or lose coverage (increase FNw). A genuine paired gain therefore requires FP reduction to outweigh lost max-cover credit. It can lose credit for new faults close to known traces. With hidden labels unavailable, the gain is consistent with pruning weak flank mass, but neither causation nor the exact penalty change can be recovered.

k(d) = max(1 − d/300 m, 0)
TPw = Σ truth g max prediction x p(x) k(d(x,g))
FPw = Σ prediction x p(x) [1 − max truth g k(d(x,g))]
FNw = |G| − TPw
DTI = TPw / (TPw + 0.2 FPw + 0.8 FNw)
    = T / (0.2 T + 0.2 F + 0.8 |G|)

Incremental placement, with the missing term restored

For extra coverage credit c and false-positive mass f, an addition helps exactly when c(1−0.2 DTI) > 0.2 DTI f. Only for one previously uncovered truth pixel, c=w and f=1−w, is this w >0.2 DTI. At 0.2778 the isolated bar is 0.05556; the 283 m diagonal has kernel weight 0.057191 and clears it. The old universal “224 m” rule was wrong.

The old hidden-truth prevalence estimates and 0.464 ceiling are retracted. One scalar score cannot identify weighted TP, FP or hidden truth count. Holding FP and truth mass fixed would require 1.388× the TP to move from 0.2778 to 0.3774; that is conditional algebra, not a forecast.

What can plausibly improve the result?

New placement supported by independent physics, not another blanket union or ever-wider catalogue buffer. Test whole unseen structures with realistic masks and buffers, retain the strongest single-view control, and verify every A-only proposal against geological alternatives. The registered contrast prototype failed that comparison.
