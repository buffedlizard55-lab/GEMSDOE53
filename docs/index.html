3. Why the GEMSDOE32 file scored 0.2778 — and what we did about it

The 0.2778 row (h33-h33-2-b2-…-zeros) is ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED as a leaderboard row in the brief; linking that row to the exact file is not established (IR-53-02). Measured on the registry copy (evidence/gemsdoe32_measured.json): 37,654 dots, 0.73% of the footprint, median nearest-dot distance 3.0 px (the metric radius), and 0% of dots within 2 px of the mapped catalogue. Why that structure scores:

  • The metric (distance-weighted Tversky, alpha 0.2, beta 0.8, 300 m triangular kernel) takes the max over the kernel for credit but the sum for false positives: two dots 1 px apart buy one credit and two FP bills.
  • Spacing dots at the 3 px kernel radius turns a fixed dot budget into the maximum number of distinct credits (measured on our holdout: packing more than doubles credit at equal FP cost, evidence/x2_ridge_holdout.json).
  • The 2 px catalogue prune deletes dots that sit on faults the organizers already mapped — those earn no credit on a target defined as faults absent from the catalogue, so they are pure false-positive mass.
  • The plateau of the d2.8 family (0.26–0.28 across at least six independent sites) says corridor quality, not dot count, is the limiter: dots only pay where hidden faults actually are.

Can we beat 0.2778? That is only answerable with an organizer receipt (none exists here). What we can show: on the spatially-blocked hide-and-recover holdout, the H8 tip/relay corridor placement beats the family that produced those files (magnetic-ridge packing at the same budget) by +0.1057 HOLDOUT-DTI (95% CI 0.0969 to 0.1144), and beats proximity-halo placement by +0.0310. The geology is not speculative: in the Great Basin, Faulds (2013) finds ~32% of geothermal fields in relay ramps/step-overs and 22% at fault tip-lines — the exact corridors H8 targets.

Labels: every holdout number = HOLDOUT-DTI (evaluator: shared template GtContext R=3, alpha=0.2, beta=0.8; 5 folds, seed 53, 60,988 withheld positives in total; reproduction check vs the frozen x2 ridge number: delta 0.0).

5. Leakage canary (Kaufman et al. protocol)

GEMSDOE29's defect: its distance-to-catalogue feature was built from the full label raster, so it is exactly 0 on every training positive — single-feature AUC 1.0. Learn–predict separation (Kaufman, Rosset, Perlich, Stitelman, KDD 2011 / TKDD 2012) is the fix: rebuild every catalogue-derived feature from visible faults only. Our canary (evidence/x4_h8_canary.json) measures each feature alone on the hide-and-recover folds:

Gate: above 0.90 = leakage until proven otherwise. The deliberately leaky positive control (C_leaky_full_catalogue_distance) measures 1.000 — a formal reproduction of the GEMSDOE29 bug — while every H8 feature is built from visible faults only and stays under 0.80.
